<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Project Focus</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<p>Congenital heart disease is genetically complex. Sequencing studies nominate many candidate genes and variants, but the functional consequences of those discoveries often remain unclear.</p>

<p>Working with clinical and computational collaborators, we aim to connect human genetic findings to cardiac developmental mechanisms. The project focuses on determining how candidate genes and variants alter cell states, lineage decisions, regulatory networks, and tissue-level development.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Approach</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<ul class="editorial-list project-list">
<li>Prioritize candidate genes and variants from human genetics and developmental genomics</li>
<li>Use CRISPR perturbation to test gene function in cardiac cell models</li>
<li>Apply single-cell and spatial readouts to identify affected cell states and pathways</li>
<li>Use iPSC and organoid systems to model human cardiac development and disease mechanisms</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Goal</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<p>The long-term goal is to build a functional framework for interpreting congenital heart disease genetics and to identify mechanisms that can guide diagnosis, modeling, and therapeutic discovery.</p>
</div>
</section>
