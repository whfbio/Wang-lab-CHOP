<h1>Direct Cardiac Reprogramming for Heart Regeneration</h1>
<p class="editorial-page-lede">We investigate how fibroblast identity can be remodeled into induced cardiomyocyte-like cells to improve cardiac repair after injury.</p>
</header>

<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Project Focus</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<p>Cardiac injury activates fibroblasts and produces fibrotic tissue that protects the damaged heart acutely but limits long-term contractile recovery. Direct cardiac reprogramming asks whether resident fibroblasts can be redirected toward cardiomyocyte-like states and contribute to heart repair.</p>

<p>Our lab studies the regulatory logic that controls this identity conversion. We are particularly interested in compact reprogramming strategies, chromatin and transcriptional barriers, and the molecular features that distinguish partial reprogramming from more mature cardiomyocyte-like outcomes.</p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Approach</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<ul class="editorial-list project-list">
<li>Use single-cell and spatial readouts to resolve intermediate reprogramming states</li>
<li>Test candidate regulators that improve cardiomyocyte gene activation and functional maturation</li>
<li>Compare in vitro and in vivo systems to identify context-dependent barriers to conversion</li>
<li>Develop perturbation strategies that connect regulatory mechanisms to cardiac repair phenotypes</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>

<section class="editorial-section">
<h2 class="editorial-section-heading">§ Goal</h2>
<div class="editorial-panel">
<p>The long-term goal is to define practical and mechanistically grounded routes for converting scar-forming cells into therapeutically useful cardiac cells, creating new strategies for myocardial regeneration.</p>
</div>
</section>
